## Problem

AI risk frameworks increasingly emphasize measurement, monitoring, evaluation, and risk management, but incident knowledge is fragmented across companies, sectors, lawsuits, news reports, benchmarks, and private postmortems. Near misses and recurring failure patterns are especially difficult to learn from across organizational boundaries.

## Proposed intervention

Design an open AI incident and near-miss reporting standard with structured harm taxonomy, system context, evidence quality, uncertainty, affected populations, mitigations, recurrence, and privacy-preserving disclosure levels. The commons would distinguish allegations, verified incidents, near misses, and synthetic test failures rather than collapsing them.

## Theory of change

If AI failures and near misses are recorded in a comparable, evidence-graded format, then developers, auditors, researchers, and policymakers can detect recurring failure modes earlier and design evaluations around observed harms rather than only hypothetical risks.

## Falsification condition

Structured incident sharing does not improve detection or prevention of recurring failures, reporting incentives are too weak, privacy and legal constraints make useful evidence impossible to share, or the database becomes dominated by unverifiable allegations and duplicate noise.

## Smallest responsible next step

Compare existing public AI incident taxonomies and NIST risk categories, then draft a minimal schema and test it on already-public incidents without adding new allegations or personal data.
