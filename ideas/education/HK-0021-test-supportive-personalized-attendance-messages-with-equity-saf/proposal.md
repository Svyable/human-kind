## Problem

Chronic absenteeism remains widespread in U.S. K-12 education and is associated with lost instructional time and worse academic and wellbeing outcomes. Federal education data report a national chronic-absence rate of about 28% in 2022-23. Absence has many causes, including health, transportation, caregiving, disengagement, and lack of supports, so information alone will not address many barriers.

## Proposed intervention

Test a narrow, supportive version of personalized caregiver attendance messaging through schools' existing communication platforms. Messages would report recent absence counts in plain language, avoid punitive or shaming framing, provide an easy opt-out, and point families toward existing support channels. Any pilot should pre-specify checks for delivery inequality, inaccurate alerts, caregiver complaints, and subgroup effects. This submission proposes research and pilot design only; it does not authorize contacting schools or families or deploying messages.

## Theory of change

If caregivers receive accurate, timely attendance information that they may not otherwise have, together with a clear route to existing support, then some avoidable absences may decrease through reduced information gaps and earlier problem-solving. The mechanism should be rejected or narrowed where absences are driven by structural barriers that messaging cannot change, where messages are not received, or where messaging damages trust.

## Beneficiaries

- K-12 students at risk of chronic absenteeism
- Caregivers receiving school attendance communications
- School communities seeking low-cost attendance supports

## Existing work

- U.S. Department of Education chronic absenteeism overview: https://www.ed.gov/teaching-and-administration/supporting-students/chronic-absenteeism
- Institute of Education Sciences impact evaluation of parent messaging strategies: https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/evaluations/impact-evaluation-parent-messaging-strategies-student-attendance
- Harvard CEPR summary of a multi-site randomized rural attendance messaging trial: https://cepr.harvard.edu/resource/lifting-attendance-rural-districts-multi-site-trial-personalized-messaging-campaign
- American Institutes for Research summary of the earlier federal randomized elementary-school messaging study: https://www.air.org/news/press-release/new-federal-study-finds-text-messages-parents-can-reduce-chronic-school-absences
- Recent multi-district evidence reports modest average reductions in absences, not elimination of chronic absenteeism. The 2026 rural trial also reports implementation failures, substantial negative caregiver feedback in responding districts, and a subgroup estimate for Black students that the authors say warrants caution and further study. These limitations are material counterevidence against treating messaging as universally benign or sufficient.

## Falsification condition

In a preregistered, ethically reviewed comparison against usual school communication, the intervention produces no practically meaningful reduction in absence days or chronic absenteeism after accounting for delivery, or any attendance benefit is outweighed by meaningful deterioration in caregiver trust, elevated complaint/opt-out rates, inaccurate alerts, or adverse subgroup effects that cannot be mitigated. Evidence that most target absences arise from barriers that information and support routing cannot address would also substantially narrow the mechanism.

## Smallest responsible next step

Do not deploy anything. First commission a bounded Scout/Skeptic evidence review that compares the recent urban/suburban and rural randomized trials, extracts implementation details and subgroup/complaint findings, and specifies a minimum ethical pilot protocol with stop conditions for inaccurate alerts, complaints, unequal delivery, or adverse subgroup signals. Human reviewers should then decide whether a real-world pilot is warranted.
