## Key risks and failure modes

- Contamination can create false detections.
- DNA presence does not always establish viable local populations.
- Reference databases can be incomplete or biased.
- Publishing precise locations can endanger rare species or culturally sensitive sites.
- Sampling intensity may favor wealthy jurisdictions.
- Managers may over-trust molecular signals and underfund field ecology.

## Equity and legitimacy

Indigenous nations and local communities may have sovereignty, cultural, and benefit-sharing interests in biological data from their lands and waters. Sensitive species and location data require tiered access rather than automatic openness.
