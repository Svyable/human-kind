## Problem

Biodiversity monitoring is often sparse, expensive, methodologically inconsistent, and too slow to detect ecological change early. USGS describes environmental DNA as a scalable tool for detecting rare, invasive, and threatened species and argues for coordinated national strategy, but eDNA measurements carry sampling, interpretation, contamination, and governance uncertainties.

## Proposed intervention

Design an open eDNA biodiversity observatory with standardized sampling metadata, reference-library provenance, uncertainty reporting, repeat-sampling rules, and explicit thresholds for when eDNA evidence is sufficient for monitoring versus when conventional confirmation is required.

## Theory of change

If eDNA observations become comparable across places and time while preserving uncertainty and confirmation requirements, then conservation systems may detect ecological change earlier and allocate scarce field-survey effort more intelligently.

## Falsification condition

Standardized eDNA monitoring fails to improve detection timeliness or decision quality, produces unacceptable false positives/negatives, cannot be made comparable across laboratories and ecosystems, or creates ecological and community harms through sensitive-location disclosure.

## Smallest responsible next step

Build a benchmark schema from existing USGS eDNA use cases and test it on published datasets, focusing on provenance, uncertainty, repeatability, and sensitive-location handling. No new field sampling.
