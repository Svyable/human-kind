## Key risks and failure modes

- A benchmark could create false confidence about inherently uncertain biology.
- Organizations may optimize to the rehearsal rather than real resilience.
- Detailed readiness maps could expose sensitive vulnerabilities.
- High-income manufacturing assumptions could dominate the benchmark.
- Speed incentives could erode safety, evidence, or equitable access if badly designed.

## Equity and legitimacy

Readiness should include geographically distributed manufacturing, regulatory capacity, affordability, and access—not only speed in wealthy settings. Low- and middle-income country institutions need meaningful design authority in any eventual benchmark.
