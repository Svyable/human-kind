## Problem

The 100 Days Mission aims to make safe, effective, accessible vaccines available within 100 days of identifying a pandemic threat. Achieving that requires an ecosystem—platforms, assays, trials, manufacturing, regulation, supply, and equitable access—to be ready before an outbreak, yet readiness can remain abstract until a real emergency tests it.

## Proposed intervention

Create an open rehearsal and benchmark framework for the 100-day vaccine mission: simulated pathogen sequences, timed handoffs, evidence requirements, manufacturing and regulatory readiness checks, equity constraints, and after-action reports. The framework would test preparedness without creating or handling live pathogens.

## Theory of change

If the end-to-end vaccine response chain is repeatedly rehearsed against standardized, non-biological scenarios, then hidden handoff delays and capacity bottlenecks can be found and fixed before a real pandemic threat compresses the timeline.

## Beneficiaries

- Populations exposed to future epidemic and pandemic threats
- Public-health systems
- Vaccine researchers, manufacturers, and regulators
- Countries with limited rapid-response manufacturing capacity

## Falsification condition

Rehearsal scores fail to predict real operational readiness, incentivize benchmark gaming, omit critical biological or regulatory uncertainty, or divert resources from more valuable preparedness capabilities.

## Smallest responsible next step

Design a paper-only tabletop benchmark using a fictional non-biological pathogen profile and map the 100-day critical path, evidence gates, dependencies, and failure states. Do not conduct wet-lab work or handle pathogens.
