## Problem

Wastewater and environmental surveillance can provide population-level signals without requiring every infected person to seek care or testing. WHO now provides pathogen-agnostic guidance, but programs often remain fragmented by pathogen, jurisdiction, laboratory pipeline, and reporting format.

## Proposed intervention

Develop a modular, pathogen-agnostic surveillance architecture and public decision protocol that can add or retire targets, preserve uncertainty, define action thresholds, and combine wastewater signals with clinical and epidemiological data rather than treating sewage measurements as standalone truth.

## Theory of change

If surveillance infrastructure can safely reuse sampling, laboratory, metadata, and reporting layers across multiple pathogens, then public-health systems may detect emerging threats earlier and at lower marginal cost while reducing dependence on care-seeking and diagnostic availability.

## Beneficiaries

- Communities facing infectious-disease outbreaks
- Public-health agencies
- People underrepresented in clinical testing data
- Health systems needing earlier situational awareness
