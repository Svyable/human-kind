## Problem

Antimicrobial resistance is often recognized through clinical surveillance after resistant organisms are already affecting patients. WHO now provides specific guidance for wastewater and environmental surveillance of AMR, creating an opportunity to test whether environmental signals can complement clinical and One Health systems without being overinterpreted.

## Proposed intervention

Design a global sentinel architecture that combines a small number of strategically chosen wastewater/environmental sites with standardized AMR targets, metadata, uncertainty, and links to clinical and animal-health surveillance. The goal is earlier situational awareness and hypothesis generation, not automatic attribution of resistance to a community or facility.

## Theory of change

If environmental AMR signals are collected consistently and interpreted alongside clinical and One Health data, emerging resistance patterns may be detected earlier or mapped more comprehensively, enabling targeted investigation before they become larger clinical surprises.

## Beneficiaries

## Falsification condition

Environmental signals do not provide useful lead time or complementary information beyond clinical surveillance, cannot be reliably attributed or standardized, or create enough stigma, cost, or interpretive error that the surveillance harms outweigh benefits.

## Smallest responsible next step

Create a literature and standards map of environmental AMR surveillance targets and compare them with clinical surveillance outcomes, explicitly identifying where environmental signals have and have not shown actionable lead time. No new sampling.
