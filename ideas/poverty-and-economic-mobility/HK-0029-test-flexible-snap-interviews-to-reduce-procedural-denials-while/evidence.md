## Sources and comparable work supplied at intake

- https://www.nber.org/papers/w31239 — randomized field experiment with about 65,000 Los Angeles SNAP applicants; reports higher approval and longer-run participation with flexible applicant-initiated interviews.
- https://www.nber.org/reporter/2024number3/snap-eligibility-enforcement-and-program-adoption — accessible summary of the Los Angeles flexible-interview experiment and the role of missed interviews in procedural denials.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/snap/state/interview-toolkit/introduction/core-requirements — current USDA description of SNAP interview requirements, privacy obligations, and state operational flexibilities.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/snap/assessment-interview — USDA study assessing the contribution of interviews to eligibility and benefit determinations; relevant prior work on the accuracy/access tradeoff.
- https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2024-31632.pdf — official Federal Register public-inspection copy of USDA's planned randomized evaluation of the SNAP interview requirement, explicitly including access, administrative efficiency, costs, and benefit accuracy.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/snap/qc/caper — USDA case and procedural error-rate data, relevant to measuring both access failures and administrative accuracy.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/snap/understanding-rates-causes-and-costs-churning-supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-snap — USDA research on SNAP churn and the burdens imposed on households and administering agencies when participants exit and re-enter.

## Central falsifier

A well-implemented comparison would substantially revise or reject the mechanism if flexible interview access does not reduce missed-interview procedural denials or improve timely completion; if any access gains are offset by materially worse payment accuracy or case-processing errors; if call wait times, abandonment, staffing requirements, or fraud-prevention steps merely relocate rather than reduce administrative burden; or if the flexible channel systematically excludes applicants with limited phone access, limited English proficiency, disabilities, unstable housing, or other access constraints.
