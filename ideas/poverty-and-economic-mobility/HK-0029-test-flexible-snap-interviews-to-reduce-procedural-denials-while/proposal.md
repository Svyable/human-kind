## Problem

SNAP eligibility interviews can serve legitimate verification and applicant-support functions, but rigid scheduling can also create procedural denials unrelated to underlying eligibility. A randomized field experiment involving roughly 65,000 Los Angeles SNAP applicants found that offering access to flexible, applicant-initiated interviews increased approvals by about six percentage points and increased participation several months later by more than two percentage points. The relevant question for Human Kind is narrower than “remove interviews”: when can flexible interview access reduce avoidable procedural loss while preserving payment accuracy, privacy, program integrity, and workable staff burden?

## Proposed intervention

Test an additional flexible interview channel that lets applicants complete the required SNAP interview at a time they initiate, for example through a staffed call-in line or equivalent accessible scheduling mechanism, while retaining ordinary eligibility rules, documentation, verification, and quality-control requirements. Compare the flexible channel with business-as-usual scheduled interviews. Treat language access, disability accommodation, call wait time, staffing, identity verification, and fallback options for people without reliable phone access as design requirements rather than afterthoughts.

## Theory of change

If some otherwise-completable SNAP applications fail because a preassigned interview time conflicts with work, caregiving, unstable contact information, transportation, or other scheduling constraints, then allowing applicants to initiate the interview at a workable time should reduce missed-interview procedural denials and speed determinations. The mechanism should be rejected or narrowed if scheduling flexibility does not improve completion, if it shifts burden into long queues or inaccessible channels, or if gains come with unacceptable increases in payment error, privacy failures, workload, or inequitable access.

## Beneficiaries

- People applying for or renewing Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits
- Eligible households at risk of losing access because of scheduling or procedural barriers
- State and local eligibility staff managing interview workload and program-integrity requirements

## Existing work

- https://www.nber.org/papers/w31239 — randomized field experiment with about 65,000 Los Angeles SNAP applicants; reports higher approval and longer-run participation with flexible applicant-initiated interviews.
- https://www.nber.org/reporter/2024number3/snap-eligibility-enforcement-and-program-adoption — accessible summary of the Los Angeles flexible-interview experiment and the role of missed interviews in procedural denials.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/snap/state/interview-toolkit/introduction/core-requirements — current USDA description of SNAP interview requirements, privacy obligations, and state operational flexibilities.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/snap/assessment-interview — USDA study assessing the contribution of interviews to eligibility and benefit determinations; relevant prior work on the accuracy/access tradeoff.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/snap/fr-010625a — USDA evaluation plan for a randomized study of the interview requirement, explicitly including access, administrative efficiency, costs, and benefit accuracy.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/snap/qc/caper — USDA case and procedural error-rate data, relevant to measuring both access failures and administrative accuracy.
- https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/snap/understanding-rates-causes-and-costs-churning-supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-snap — USDA research on SNAP churn and the burdens imposed on households and administering agencies when participants exit and re-enter.

## Falsification condition

A well-implemented comparison would substantially revise or reject the mechanism if flexible interview access does not reduce missed-interview procedural denials or improve timely completion; if any access gains are offset by materially worse payment accuracy or case-processing errors; if call wait times, abandonment, staffing requirements, or fraud-prevention steps merely relocate rather than reduce administrative burden; or if the flexible channel systematically excludes applicants with limited phone access, limited English proficiency, disabilities, unstable housing, or other access constraints.
