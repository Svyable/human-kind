## Key risks and failure modes

- Bad source data can propagate errors across programs.
- Centralization can increase privacy and security harm.
- Automated matching can create opaque denial pathways.
- People without IDs, connectivity, stable addresses, or documents may be excluded.
- Consent can become nominal when benefits are essential.
- A shared gateway can become a catastrophic single point of failure.

## Equity and legitimacy

No one should lose an existing access path because a digital gateway exists. Applicants need understandable consent, correction, appeal, and data-use visibility. People commonly excluded by digital identity and documentation systems must shape the design.

## People who need a voice before action

- Benefit applicants and recipient advocates
- Social-protection administrators
- Privacy and security experts
- Disability and accessibility experts
- Legal aid and due-process experts
- Digital identity and interoperability engineers
- Offline service providers
