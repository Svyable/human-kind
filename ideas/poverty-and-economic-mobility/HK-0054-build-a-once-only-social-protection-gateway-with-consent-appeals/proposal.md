## Problem

People often have to repeat registration, eligibility, documentation, and payment setup across fragmented social programs. The World Bank describes interoperable social registries and digital delivery systems as a way to reduce fragmentation, but integration can also concentrate surveillance, exclusion, and data-governance risks.

## Proposed intervention

Design a once-only application gateway where a person can submit core information once, see which programs may be relevant, consent to specific data reuse, correct records, appeal decisions, and use a fully functional offline or assisted path. Programs retain their legal eligibility rules; the gateway reduces procedural duplication rather than silently making eligibility decisions.

## Theory of change

If repeated administrative steps are consolidated while preserving program-specific due process, consent, correction, and non-digital access, then eligible households may receive support faster with less paperwork and agencies may reduce duplicate processing.

## Beneficiaries

- People eligible for multiple social programs
- Low-income households facing repeated paperwork
- People with disabilities or limited digital access
- Public agencies administering benefits

## Falsification condition

Integrated gateways increase exclusion, erroneous denials, surveillance, or breach impact; do not reduce applicant burden or processing time; or create single points of failure that outweigh interoperability benefits.

## Smallest responsible next step

Map 3-5 real multi-program delivery systems and identify which data and steps are safely reusable, which must remain program-specific, and where consent, correction, appeal, and offline fallback are mandatory. Do not build or connect a live registry.
