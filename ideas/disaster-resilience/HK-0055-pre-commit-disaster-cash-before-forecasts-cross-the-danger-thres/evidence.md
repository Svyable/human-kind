## Claim separation

The dossier should not treat one broad proposition as established. It contains at least two distinct claims:

1. **Timing claim:** in some flood settings, delivering cash sooner can improve welfare relative to delivering the same assistance later. The Bangladesh/Nepal RCT directly tests this timing question and reports gains in food security and psychosocial well-being from timely cash.
2. **Trigger-architecture claim:** a specified forecast threshold, financing rule, targeting system, and delivery rail can reliably release assistance to the right people with acceptable false-positive and false-negative costs. This remains a context-specific hypothesis and is not established by evidence that earlier cash can help.

The second claim requires explicit forecast-skill and decision analysis. Trigger evaluation should distinguish hits, false alarms, misses, and correct rejections, and compare the cost of acting in vain with the harm from failing to act rather than optimizing forecast accuracy alone.

## Evidence calibration

This dossier remains at `evidence_level: hypothesis`. The evidence for the timing mechanism is stronger than the evidence for the proposed trigger and financing architecture. ## Central falsifier

For a specified hazard and geography, narrow or reject the proposal if a retrospective trigger simulation shows that realistic thresholds produce unacceptable false-negative harm, unaffordable false-positive activations, insufficient lead time for payment delivery, or little welfare advantage over a well-prepared rapid post-shock transfer.
