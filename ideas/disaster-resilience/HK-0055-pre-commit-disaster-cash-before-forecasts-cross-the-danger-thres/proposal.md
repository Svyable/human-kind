## Problem

Humanitarian assistance often arrives after households have already lost assets, income, food security, or safe mobility. WFP's anticipatory-action evidence includes cases and evaluations where assistance was triggered before forecasted hazards, but scaling requires credible triggers, pre-arranged finance, delivery readiness, and protection against false alarms and exclusion.

## Proposed intervention

Design a pre-committed anticipatory cash mechanism in which funding, recipient safeguards, delivery rails, trigger thresholds, and post-event evaluation are agreed before a hazard season. Release would occur only when a transparent forecast threshold is met, with explicit provisions for false positives, false negatives, appeals, and cases where local evidence conflicts with the modeled trigger.

## Claims under test

This proposal deliberately separates two claims that require different evidence:

1. **Earlier-delivery claim:** when a damaging event occurs, receiving cash earlier can improve household welfare relative to receiving the same support substantially later.
2. **Trigger-design claim:** for a specified hazard and geography, a particular forecast threshold and delivery design can produce enough lead time and welfare value to justify its false-alarm, miss, exclusion, and activation costs.

Evidence that supports the first claim does not automatically validate the second.

## Theory of change

If finance and delivery are prepared before a predictable shock and released on a validated forecast trigger, households may protect assets, evacuate, buy essentials, and avoid harmful coping strategies earlier than under post-disaster assistance. The mechanism only works if forecast skill, targeting, payment readiness, and market conditions jointly produce useful lead time without unacceptable exclusion or wasted activation.

## Falsification condition

For a specified hazard and geography, narrow or reject the proposal if realistic thresholds produce unacceptable false-negative harm, unaffordable false-positive activations, insufficient lead time for payment delivery, or little welfare advantage over a well-prepared rapid post-shock transfer.

## Smallest responsible next step

Choose one historical hazard and geography with accessible forecast, impact, and delivery data. Predeclare several candidate thresholds, then retrospectively calculate hits, false alarms, misses, correct rejections, lead time, population reached, and approximate activation cost. Overlay those results with the available welfare evidence on earlier versus later cash. The goal is to identify whether any decision region jointly satisfies forecast skill, delivery lead time, equity, and welfare value—not to optimize a universal trigger. No money is moved and no recipient list is created.
