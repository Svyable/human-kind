## Problem

Satellite systems can now detect very large methane emissions globally, but detection does not reliably become mitigation. UNEP reports that MARS notifications have enabled verified mitigation cases while response rates still lag in many jurisdictions. The gap is increasingly an accountability and response-design problem, not only a sensing problem.

## Proposed intervention

Design an open, auditable methane-response protocol that pairs public super-emitter alerts with acknowledgement deadlines, investigation status, mitigation evidence, uncertainty, and closure criteria. Test the protocol on historical public MARS events before proposing any live integration.

## Theory of change

If high-confidence methane alerts are converted into transparent, time-bounded response records with verification and escalation states, then fewer actionable leaks may disappear into institutional handoffs and the time from detection to mitigation may fall.

## Falsification condition

Historical analysis shows that public response-state tracking does not predict faster investigation or mitigation, produces substantial false attribution or perverse incentives, or adds administrative burden without improving closure of actionable emissions.

## Smallest responsible next step

Build a retrospective benchmark from public MARS events and test whether a minimal response-state schema can represent detection, acknowledgement, investigation, mitigation, verification, dispute, and closure without inventing missing facts. Do not contact operators or regulators.
