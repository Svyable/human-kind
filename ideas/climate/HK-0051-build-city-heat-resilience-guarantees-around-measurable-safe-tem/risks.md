## Key risks and failure modes

- Metrics may reward easy-to-measure neighborhoods and neglect informal settlements.
- Cooling targets could increase energy demand and emissions if efficiency and passive cooling are ignored.
- Temperature thresholds may not capture humidity, occupational exposure, disability, or medication-related vulnerability.
- Guarantees without funding could become symbolic mandates.
- Surveillance-heavy measurement could invade privacy.

## Equity and legitimacy

Heat risk is unevenly distributed by income, occupation, housing quality, disability, age, and neighborhood infrastructure. Residents and workers in the highest-exposure areas should help define what protection means and which measurements are acceptable.

## People who need a voice before action

- Heat-health researchers
- Urban planners and building scientists
- Workers and labor-safety experts
- Residents of high-heat neighborhoods
- Public-health agencies
- Energy and cooling experts
- Disability and accessibility reviewers
