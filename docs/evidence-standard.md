# Evidence Standard

## Claim discipline

For material claims, distinguish:

- **observed fact** — directly supported by a cited source or dataset;
- **inference** — reasoned conclusion from evidence;
- **expert judgment** — attributed interpretation by a qualified person or institution;
- **assumption** — working premise used for design;
- **hypothesis** — testable claim not yet adequately supported;
- **model output** — generated estimate or synthesis that requires verification.

## Source preference

Prefer primary sources, high-quality systematic reviews, transparent datasets, official statistics, preregistered or replicated studies, and authoritative standards when appropriate. Secondary sources can orient research but should not silently substitute for primary evidence on consequential claims.

## Citation requirements

Record title, author or institution, date when known, stable link or identifier, and the claim the source supports. Do not cite a source merely because it is topically related.

## Calibration

State uncertainty, limitations, conflicts of interest, population/context mismatch, and important counterevidence. Absence of evidence is not automatically evidence of absence.

## Novelty

Search for comparable interventions and existing institutions before claiming a proposal is novel. Rediscovery can still be useful, but it should be described accurately.

## Agent-generated research

Agents may accelerate discovery and synthesis, but source existence, quotations, quantitative claims, and causal assertions require human-verifiable citations before they can support promotion or external action.
