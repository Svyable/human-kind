# Theory of Change

Human Kind assumes that many socially valuable ideas fail not because ideas are scarce, but because prior work is fragmented, claims are weakly calibrated, risks are discovered late, and learning is poorly preserved.

If contributors convert broad proposals into versioned dossiers; connect claims to evidence; expose assumptions to skeptical review; involve affected people; design small reversible tests; and preserve evaluation history, then promising interventions should become easier to distinguish from attractive but weak ideas.

The repository does not assume that better documentation alone produces social benefit. Real-world benefit requires capable owners, legitimate participation, resources, governance, and context-specific implementation.

## Intended outputs

- better-scoped proposals;
- reusable evidence maps;
- explicit risk registers;
- testable pilot designs;
- documented negative results;
- transparent decision trails.

## Intended outcomes

- less duplicated research;
- earlier detection of weak assumptions and harms;
- more credible transitions from idea to ethical pilot;
- better institutional memory about what was tried and why.

## Failure conditions

The model weakens if the repository rewards volume, status, rhetoric, or agent-generated fluency over evidence; if affected people are excluded; if pilots outrun governance; or if archival incentives hide negative findings.
