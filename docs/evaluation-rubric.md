# Evaluation Rubric

Scores support structured discussion; they do not replace judgment. Publish the component scores and a narrative rationale. Do not use a single aggregate “humanity score” as the primary interface.

Use a 1–5 scale for each dimension, where 1 means major deficiencies or unknowns and 5 means unusually strong support.

| Dimension | Weight | Review question |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Problem importance | 20% | Is the problem material, neglected, and well-scoped? |
| Evidence | 20% | Are claims sourced, calibrated, and separated from hypotheses? |
| Tractability | 15% | Is there a credible route to a testable action? |
| Expected impact | 15% | If successful, how meaningful and durable is the benefit? |
| Equity and legitimacy | 15% | Are affected people represented and burdens fairly considered? |
| Safety and misuse | 10% | Are failure modes, dual use, reversibility, and safeguards addressed? |
| Open collaboration | 5% | Can others verify, reproduce, critique, or extend the work? |

## Review output

Every substantive evaluation should record:

- component scores or `unknown`;
- evidence supporting each score;
- major disagreements or uncertainty;
- blocking concerns;
- recommended status;
- smallest responsible next action;
- conditions that would cause the recommendation to change.

High scores do not authorize implementation. Ethics, legal obligations, stakeholder legitimacy, owner capability, and context still govern action.
